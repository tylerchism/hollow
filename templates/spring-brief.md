# Spring Brief Template — Standard v1

**Purpose:** Required input for all Spring invocations. Also serves as Editor's target spec — Editor evaluates output against this brief, not against general quality standards.

---

## The Template

```
SPRING BRIEF
============
audience:
  who:
  knowledge_level:
  what_they_want:

format:

voice:

length:

publication_context:

angle:

raw_material: |

constraints:
  avoid:
  must_include:
  cta:
```

---

## Field Definitions + Example Values

**`audience.who`**
The specific reader, not a demographic. Name the person or archetype.
> *Example:* `"Someone already farming or gardening who's skeptical of hype but open to evidence"`

**`audience.knowledge_level`**
What do they already know? What will bore them? What will lose them?
> *Example:* `"Knows basic soil biology, hasn't heard of keyline design, doesn't need the term explained but needs the concept grounded"`

**`audience.what_they_want`**
Why are they reading? What do they leave with?
> *Example:* `"A specific technique they can test this season, not a philosophy lecture"`

---

**`format`**
Choose one: `essay` | `newsletter section` | `thread` | `social post` | `script` | `listicle` | `how-to` | `op-ed`
> *Example:* `newsletter section`

---

**`voice`**
Register + constraints. Tyler has a house voice (direct, earned confidence, no hedging, occasional dry humor, first-person). Override or specify variants here.
> *Example:* `"Tyler's standard voice. No rhetorical questions. Don't start with a statistic."`

---

**`length`**
Hard numbers, not soft descriptors. No "short" or "long."
> *Example:* `"700–950 words"` or `"5–7 tweets, each standalone"` or `"under 200 words"`

---

**`publication_context`**
Where does this live? Platform conventions that constrain the piece.
> *Example:* `"Substack newsletter — mid-section piece, readers have already read an intro, this isn't the hook"` or `"Twitter/X thread — no intro tweet, start with the claim"`

---

**`angle`**
The single sentence this piece is built around. One claim. If you can't write it in one sentence, the brief isn't ready yet.
> *Example:* `"Most soil carbon advice optimizes for sequestration metrics, not for the biology that makes sequestration durable."`

---

**`raw_material`**
What Spring is working from. Paste Tap output, rough notes, or a link. If blank, Spring invents — that's usually a problem.
> *Example:* `[paste of Tap research summary]`

---

**`constraints.avoid`**
Specific things not to include or ways not to open.
> *Example:* `"Avoid the word 'regenerative' in the opening paragraph."`

**`constraints.must_include`**
Required elements that survive editing.
> *Example:* `"Must mention the Yeomans plow by name at least once"`

**`constraints.cta`**
Call to action if applicable. Leave blank if none.
> *Example:* `"End with a pointer to the course — soft, not salesy"`

---

## Minimal Valid Brief

Not every task needs every field. This is the floor:

```
SPRING BRIEF
============
audience.who: [one line]
format: [one word]
voice: [one line or "Tyler standard"]
length: [number range]
publication_context: [one line]
angle: [one sentence]
```

If `angle` is blank, Tarn should push back before routing to Spring.

---

## How Editor Uses This

Editor receives the brief alongside Spring's output. One question per field:

- **audience** — Would this reader finish it? Would it bore or lose them anywhere?
- **format** — Does the structure match the stated format?
- **voice** — Does any sentence break the register?
- **length** — Is the output within range? If not, what's the fat?
- **publication_context** — Does the piece fit where it's landing?
- **angle** — Is the stated angle legible by paragraph 2? Does it hold through the end?
- **constraints** — Were the hard rules respected?

Editor flags deviations. It doesn't rewrite toward generic quality — it rewrites toward *this brief*.
